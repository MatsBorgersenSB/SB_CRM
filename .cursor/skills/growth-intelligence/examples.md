# Growth Intelligence — good vs bad

## 1. Unfundable plant

**Facts:** Open deal, Prospect. No funding path. Offtake unknown.

**Bad:** “Push machinery close this quarter. Book a demo.”

**Good:** Offer `walk_away` or `paid_feasibility` only if they will pay to test bankability. “A plant that cannot be funded is not a machinery campaign. Impact: engineering time on a dead CAPEX steals live bids.”

## 2. Missing economic buyer

**Facts:** Technical evaluator on the roster. Buying role Unknown for everyone else. Reactor in scope.

**Bad:** Send the turnkey proposal.

**Good:** This week: name the economic buyer. Offer stays `engineering` or `watch` until capital coverage exists. Hand live-record capture to SmartAssist; you still own the bet.

## 3. Buy-from company in whitespace

**Facts:** CompanyTypes = Vendor. No opportunity.

**Bad:** Whitespace cell “un-pitched turnkey — Create Opportunity.”

**Good:** Do not run sell-to whitespace. Hand off to SmartAssist: supplier follow-through. Posture first.

## 4. Competitor in the hearing

**Facts:** Customer mail mentions PYREG. We have an open overlapping deal.

**Bad:** Invent a five-force SWOT. Recommend we “also sell biochar” because they do.

**Good:** Record the hearing as observed. Tie it to our deal. Choose our counter-offer from the **catalog** (usually qualification or machinery with a named difference). Biochar remains something we do not sell.

## 5. Empty offerings on a sell-to deal

**Bad:** Assume Complete Turnkey Pyrolysis System.

**Good:** Offerings unknown (`offering-intelligence.ts`). Next: user selects catalog SKUs. Then pick `paid_feasibility` vs `machinery` from evidence, not from a guess.
